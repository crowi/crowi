Everything about how you sign in lives under **Settings → Password / API tokens / Linked accounts**.

## Changing your password

You can change your login password here. On an account that has no password yet — one that only signs in through an external account, for example — there is no "current password" field; you simply choose a new one.

Changing your password **signs out every other login session on the account**. If you suspect someone else has a session, changing your password is how you evict them.

- The tab you made the change in stays signed in. Other browsers, other devices and other tabs return to the login screen on their next action.
- **Personal access tokens and authorized apps are unaffected.** They are separate credentials and are revoked individually from the sections below.
- Any password-reset link already in someone's inbox stops working.
- A pending email-address change is cancelled (request it again if you still want it).

If you forget your password, the login screen can email you a reset link. A reset link expires after **one hour** and works **only once**. It is also bound to the address it was sent to, so if your account's email address changes after the link was sent, the old link stops working — request a new one.

The same applies when an administrator resets your password from the admin panel: every session is signed out and any outstanding reset link is invalidated.

## Personal access tokens

A **personal access token (PAT)** lets an external tool use the Crowi API as you. The [CLI (command line)](./cli) and [Using Crowi from AI tools (MCP)](./mcp) both authenticate with one.

- You can hold **several tokens**. Each has its own name, scopes (what it may do) and expiry (30 / 90 / 365 days, or never).
- The token itself (`crowi_pat_…`) is shown **once, at creation**. The server stores only a hash, so it can never be shown again — copy it before closing the dialog.
- The list shows each token's scopes, expiry and last use, so you can spot one nothing uses and revoke it. Revoking one token does not affect the others.
- Admin-only scopes cannot be attached to a token.

<Callout type="warn">
  A token is as powerful as your password. If one might have leaked, revoke it and issue a new one. **Changing your password does not revoke tokens** — and revoking a token does not touch your login sessions.
</Callout>

## Authorized apps

External apps you connected over OAuth — the CLI, the mobile app — are listed here and can be revoked. **Web login sessions do not appear in this list**; they are a separate kind of credential.

Each row shows the app name, its scopes, when it was authorized, when it last refreshed, and when it expires. **"Last refreshed" is when the app renewed its credentials, not when it last called the API.**

Revoking a row stops that app from renewing its credentials. **An access token it already holds keeps working until it expires (one hour by default).** To lock an app out immediately, wait out that hour or change your password and sign in again.

- Right after a refresh, the same app may briefly show two rows. Reloading the page collapses it back to one.
- If the same app refreshed from two places at nearly the same time, both rows can persist. Revoking one leaves the other, so revoke both when you notice.

## Linking an external account

When your administrator has enabled external sign-in (Google, for example), you can attach an external account to your Crowi account from the same screen.

1. Press **Link** and you are taken to the provider's sign-in screen.
2. After authenticating you come back to Crowi and a confirmation dialog appears.
3. Press **Link** once more to finish.

If you leave the flow part-way it expires within a few minutes; start again from the beginning. Linking never overwrites the email address on your Crowi account.

<Callout type="info">
  **Once any external account is linked, your email address becomes read-only.** Your way in now lives outside Crowi, so someone who could swap the address would also capture the confirmation mail — and with it your only way back into the account.
</Callout>

### Unlinking

To unlink, you must be able to sign in with a password. Unlinking is refused when you have no password set, or when password sign-in is disabled for the whole instance. Only the presence of a password counts: having another provider linked does not qualify.

If you cannot unlink because you have no password, ask an administrator. For an account with no password, an administrator can issue one and then unlink.
