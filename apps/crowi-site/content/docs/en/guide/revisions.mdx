title: Revisions and rollback
description: Page history, diffs and rolling back to past versions
---

Every time you save a page, the body at that moment is recorded as a
**revision**. A Crowi page itself always points at the latest revision, and
all past revisions remain as history. This lets you trace "when, who, and
what changed" after the fact, and roll back to a past state if needed.

## What a revision is

A revision is a snapshot of a page body, created one per save. Each revision
holds the following.

- The page path
- The body (Markdown)
- The author
- The creation timestamp
- The saver (`savedBy`) and contributors (`contributors`)

`savedBy` and `contributors` are recorded when you save during realtime
collaborative editing. `savedBy` is the user who pressed the Save button, and
`contributors` lists the users who were present on the same page during that
save cycle. See [Realtime collaborative editing](./realtime-editing) for
details.

> **Note**: Older revisions created in Crowi 1.x have no `savedBy` /
> `contributors`. In that case, only the author is shown, as before.

## Viewing history

A page's history can be viewed on the history screen at
`/_history?path=<page path>`. The history screen lists the page's revisions
newest first.

Each row in the list shows the following.

| Column | Content |
| ------ | ------- |
| From / To | Radio buttons to pick the two revisions to compare |
| Author | The revision's saver. Contributors are shown alongside when present |
| Created | When the revision was created |
| Revision | Identifying information for the revision |

A revision created by collaborative editing displays the saver combined with
the contributors, like "Alice (with Bob, Carol)".

## Viewing diffs

On the history screen you can pick any two revisions and view the **diff**.

- When you open the history screen, the "latest revision" and "the revision
  just before it" are selected by default, so the most recent change is shown
  immediately.
- Pick the pair of revisions you want to compare with the From / To radio
  buttons and press the "Compare" button to refresh the diff view below.
- For a page with only one revision (a page that has never been updated since
  creation), the single first revision is shown.

## Viewing a specific revision

You can open a specific past revision directly with the query string
`?revision_id=<revisionId>` on a page URL. When the revision shown is
**not the latest revision** for that page, a **stale-revision banner**
appears at the top of the page view, warning that you are looking at
an older snapshot and offering a link back to the latest version.

The stale-revision banner also suppresses social affordances such as
the presence row and the page-action toolbar, since those operations
only make sense against the latest revision.

`?revision_id=` works the same way not only on normal pages but also on
**portal pages** (directory pages whose path ends with `/`). When you
open a portal at a past revision, the same stale-revision banner appears
above the portal body.

## Rolling back to a past version

When you want to return to the content of a past revision, perform a
**rollback**.

A rollback re-saves the body of a past revision as a new revision. Rather than
rewinding history, it saves the past body again, so the page's latest state
is replaced with the past content.

The stale-revision banner shown when you open a past version with
`?revision_id=` has a **"Revert to this version"** button.

- Pressing the button performs the rollback in place, with no confirmation
  dialog, and then navigates you to the rolled-back latest page (the URL
  without `?revision_id=`). There is no confirmation step because a rollback
  is non-destructive (it only adds one new revision without erasing history)
  and can always be redone.
- Even if another user updates the page while you have an older version open,
  the rollback is always stacked **on top of the server-side latest
  revision**. It never raises a conflict error, and the whole history is
  preserved.
- You can roll back from the same banner and the same button on both normal
  pages and portal pages.

A rollback does not erase the revision history up to that point — it simply
adds one new revision. This means that even in a "I rolled back, but actually
want to undo that" situation, you can switch again as yet another new revision.

> **Tip**: Because history stacks up in a straight line, you can roll back
> safely even when you notice a mistaken edit. "Only move forward, never
> erase history" is Crowi's history model.
